Essay · Coaching notes · 2025–2026

What the training log taught me.

The programme was the starting point. The interesting work happened when the session did not match it.

I came to coaching through my own training. Since 2025 I have worked with one lifter, first using my own programmes and later plans developed with ChatGPT from the literature. Writing a plausible session was only part of that work. The decisions that followed — reducing work, clarifying an instruction, or keeping a technical issue open — deserve a closer look.

This essay follows those decisions through the available records. It is not a complete session-by-session diary. The athlete is unnamed and has agreed to this publication; the original correspondence is omitted.

A heavier bar needs context.

The summer 2026 deadlift reports form a neat upward line. Presented alone, it would be easy to call it a strength gain. But the prescriptions moved from fours to triples to doubles, and the final load had already been lifted in competition. What the graph shows clearly is a change in the work.

Some mistakes were in the text. Others happened after it.

A contradictory stop rule and an unclear set count are defects in a prescription. Ignoring an existing RPE fallback is a different problem. I want to keep that distinction when describing what improved: a clearer plan is useful, but it is not proof of a better outcome. “Prescription corrected” below means the instruction changed; whether it improved the athlete's outcome is a separate question. Exchanges are paraphrased, not reproduced.

A1Reducing the work became part of the planPrescription corrected
Before

22 May 2026: bench 100 kg for 5 × 4, close-grip 90 kg for 4 × 6, plus incline dumbbells and upper-body accessories.

After

3 June 2026, with low readiness: bench 100 kg for 4 × 4, close-grip 80–85 kg for 2 × 5; triceps optional, dumbbell pressing removed. A 105 kg alternative available after warm-up.

Assessment

The two base barbell prescriptions change from 44 to 26 reps and 4,160 to 2,400–2,450 kg of planned tonnage. The athlete reported 105 kg, so the base option is not claimed as completed.

A2A contradictory stop rule was removedPrescription corrected
Before

27 July 2026: stop if pain increases — but another branch says to do two lighter sets at 95 kg if pain increases after the first work set. The actions conflict.

After

29 August 2026: end bench at any stage if pain occurs; do not reduce weight just to finish the session. September 2026 repeats an explicit stop condition.

Assessment

The text logic was corrected. Reported improvement by 12 September 2026 cannot be attributed to this change alone.

The limits of this audit. High volume alone does not prove a programming error. A missing report does not prove a missed session. Later improvement does not isolate the effect of one exercise. This audit evaluates the recorded decisions and feedback.

Technical feedback takes longer.

Foot pressure, bench set-up and deadlift lockout recur across the record. There are encouraging sessions, followed by similar comments later. That recurrence matters: it prevents a single good report from becoming a success story the record cannot support.

What is working

Competition strength. Competition records establish successful bench and deadlift performances; I describe the meet deadlift as having reserve, without quantifying it.

Response to feedback. Specific better sessions: bench stability on 2 March 2026, deadlift lockout on 5 April 2026, squat execution on 11 September 2026.

What remains open

Bench set-up and leg drive. Positive sessions coexist with recurring set-up and bar-path criticism. A good day is not yet a repeatable skill.

Lockout, bracing, following the cap. Deadlift knee lockout and squat control remain technical tasks; the June 2026 RPE episode shows the gap between a valid rule and its execution.

Five technical threads · observations over time, not controlled experiments — more than one thing changed

Squat · foot contactLocal effect

Footwear helped locally.

Problem. Weight shifted to the heels and the left knee drifted inward.

Intervention. Removing soft shoes, stance changes and unilateral work; later heeled versus flat shoes.

Observed. Better stability in specific sessions; knee movement later recurred. No single footwear change solved the block.

Deadlift · set-up & lockoutStill in progress

A workable pull. An unfinished lockout.

Problem. Conventional pulls repeatedly showed weak pre-tension and early hip rise.

Intervention. Rotated conventional and sumo, bands, slow lowering and pauses; later a return to sumo and dead-stop resets.

Observed. The heavier load had already been established in competition; September 2026 returned to it within a new cycle, and knee lockout still drew criticism. No isolated effect of the stance change is established.

Bench · set-upNot yet consistent

Strength was there. Repeatability was not.

Problem. Scapular position, elbow movement and shifting feet disrupted stability.

Intervention. Floor press, pauses, bands, grip and footwear changes; later full-foot contact and a revised set-up sequence.

Observed. Stable sessions in March 2026 and improved leg drive in July 2026 did not transfer consistently; September 2026 again flags a zigzag bar path and weak leg drive.

Squat · bracing & depthOne improved session

From too high to too deep.

Problem. Depth, rushing between reps and loss of trunk tension recur. By September 2026, fear of missing depth leads to excessive depth.

Intervention. Paused squats, breathing and bracing cues; a lighter filmed depth check was suggested.

Observed. The 11 September 2026 160 kg squat was praised, with residual shaking and excess depth. Completion of the proposed 100 kg filming check is not confirmed.

Load · forearmsPartial improvement

Less extra work. Better reported symptoms.

Problem. Forearm discomfort recurs in July and August 2026, affecting several lifts.

Intervention. Removed direct grip work, reduced accessories, used straps and clarified bench stop conditions.

Observed. By 11–12 September 2026 the athlete reports feeling much better. This coincides with several adjustments; it does not establish a single cause or a medical outcome.
