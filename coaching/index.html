Coaching · 2025–2026

What I changed while coaching one lifter.

I started with programmes. The harder work was deciding what to change after a real session.

Since 2025 I have coached one athlete: first with my own programmes, then with plans developed with ChatGPT from the literature. My responsibility stayed the same — adapt the work to the person, read the feedback, and own the next decision. Below are three decisions from that work, with different outcomes.

Three decisions. Different outcomes.

These are small, specific examples. One changed a prescription. One exposed a gap between a rule and its use. One is still unfinished.

Adapting the workMay–June 2026

I reduced the session, not just the weight.

In May 2026 the bench session combined the main lift, close-grip work and several accessories. When readiness was low in June 2026, I reduced the work sets and removed incline dumbbell pressing. The plan also carried an alternative working weight to pick after the warm-up.

What I take from it: the useful adjustment was making the session smaller and more specific. A new number on the bar would not have explained the whole change.

Plan versus executionJune 2026

The fallback existed. It still wasn't used.

The squat plan prescribed 130 kg, an RPE ceiling of 8.5, and a reduction to 125 kg if the work became too heavy. In the 5 June 2026 reports the load stayed at 130 kg. I rated the effort at 9–9.5 from the video and said it should have come down.

Prescribed decision

130 → 125 kg

Reduce the remaining work if the effort limit is reached.

What the report showed

130 kg remained

The limit was written down; following it was a separate problem.

What I take from it: I cannot describe this as a missing fallback. The next problem to solve was how to make the agreed decision easier to follow during the session.

Technical feedbackMarch–September 2026

A better session wasn't a finished fix.

Bench set-up was a recurring theme: upper-back position, leg drive and bar path. There were better sessions — more stability in March 2026, better leg drive in July 2026. By September 2026 an uneven bar path and weak leg drive were back in the feedback. I tried pauses, changes to set-up and more explicit tempo instructions.

What I take from it: the record supports local improvements. It does not support calling the issue permanently solved, or attributing the result to one exercise. This remains open; the criterion is repeatable execution across sessions, not one encouraging video.
